## Executive Summary — why Australia wins

1. **The 60% arbitrage.** Singapore's 60% Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty dwarfs Australia's state foreign surcharges (0–9%). Australian state surcharges are a fraction of what a Singaporean investor pays at home — and many benefits (no estate tax, no sale withholding, freehold title) simply do not exist in comparator markets.
2. **Open for new builds — the only lawful door.** Foreign persons are banned from purchasing *established* dwellings from 1 April 2025 to 30 June 2029 (ATO). Buying NEW is the primary lawful pathway for most foreign non-residents — the regulatory framework itself channels foreign capital into new developments, which is precisely the product on offer.
3. **No estate or inheritance tax** on Australian property (vs US 40% estate tax above USD 60k; UK 40% IHT).
4. **No FIRPTA-style sale withholding.** Australia's 15% Foreign Resident Capital Gains Withholding is a prepayment against the final CGT liability (creditable/refundable) — not a punitive flat tax like the US 15% FIRPTA withholding.
5. **Australia is the only major Western market fully open to foreign buyers of new dwellings.** Canada bans most foreign buyers (to 2027); the US has state-level bans (e.g. Florida SB 264); the UK adds a 2% non-resident surcharge.

## 1. The Purchase Pathway (the regulatory door)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **FIRB approval** | Foreign persons buying new dwellings need Foreign Investment Review Board (FIRB) approval — a straightforward one-off application, generally not refused for new dwellings. |
| **New-dwelling definition** | A dwelling that has never been occupied and has not been previously sold as a dwelling. Covers off-the-plan and near-new purchases. |
| **Exemption certificates** | Developers of off-the-plan projects can hold New/Near-New Dwelling Exemption Certificates — buyers in certified developments face a streamlined path (the developer reports sales; the buyer's process is simpler). |
| **Off-the-plan mechanics** | 10% deposit at exchange, balance at settlement (typically 18–36 months). Deposit held in trust. No mortgage interest during construction for the buyer. |
| **Vacant land with approval** | Foreign persons may buy vacant land with a development approval — construction must commence within 24 months. |
| **Ban on established dwellings** | 1 April 2025 – 30 June 2029 (extended from an earlier 31 March 2027 expiry). Temporary visa holders have limited exceptions (e.g. residence in the property). |

**Key insight:** the ban on established dwellings makes *new property the only lawful entry point* — this is a structural tailwind for the new-dwelling market, and it is government policy, not marketing.

## 2. Government Incentives — the full inventory

### National (FIRB / Commonwealth)

- **New dwellings = the least-restrictive FIRB category.** No ban, no foreign-buyer tax at the Commonwealth level (state surcharges apply — see below).
- **One-off application fee, not annual.** 2026-27 fees (ATO, indexed 1 July 2026): **$15,600** for a dwelling ≤ $1M; $31,300 for ≤ $2M; $62,600 for ≤ $3M. (2025-26 was $15,100 — fees index annually.)
- **Fee refunds.** 50% of the application fee is refundable if the purchase does not proceed (e.g. off-the-plan contract falls through) — a real risk-mitigation feature.
- **Developer exemption certificates** streamline the buyer process in certified new developments.
- **No Commonwealth stamp duty, no annual property tax, no estate tax.**

### State-level incentives (pick the jurisdiction for maximum benefit)

| State | Foreign surcharge duty | Off-the-plan / new-dwelling benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **WA** | 7% | **100% transfer duty concession on off-the-plan new dwellings ≤ $800,000** (capped $50k), sliding to 50% up to $900k — extended to 30 June 2028. The strongest state incentive in Australia. |
| **VIC** | 8% | Off-the-plan construction-cost deduction on duty (apartments/units/townhouses on strata lots with common property) — concession extended to late October 2026. |
| **QLD** | 8% | Standard duty applies to new dwellings; no concession for investors, but no land tax surcharge above the general regime. |
| **NSW** | 9% | Off-the-plan payment deferral for some purchasers; strongest long-run capital-growth market. |
| **ACT** | None | **No foreign surcharge at all** — the lowest-tax capital territory for foreign investors. |
| **NT** | None | **No foreign surcharge** — combined with low entry prices. |
| **SA** | 8% | Standard regime. |
| **TAS** | 8% | Standard regime. |

### Tax advantages

- **No estate / inheritance tax** on Australian property held by foreign persons (vs US 40%, UK 40%).
- **No recurring property tax** on the dwelling (US-style); Australia's land tax applies only above high thresholds, with a foreign-owner surcharge variant in some states (e.g. NSW 5% on land value from 2025 — verified Revenue NSW).
- **Depreciation benefits on new builds.** New properties maximise capital-works deductions (Division 43, ~2.5%/yr over 40 years) and plant & equipment depreciation — the newer the build, the larger the deduction pool.
- **Negative gearing is legal** (losses offset Australian-sourced income; practical benefit depends on the investor's Australian income position).
- **Double-taxation treaties** with Singapore, China and Malaysia allocate primary property-taxing rights to Australia and provide home-country foreign tax credits — no double taxation.
- **No capital controls on exit.** Sale proceeds are freely repatriable from Australia (the investor's home-country rules are the only constraint).

### Market & macro benefits

- **Structural undersupply:** the National Housing Accord targets 1.2M new homes; supply shortage is a government-acknowledged tailwind for new property.
- **Migration-driven tenant demand:** record migration, low vacancy in Sydney/Melbourne/Brisbane/Perth, gross yields ~3.5–5% on new builds.
- **Education corridor:** proximity to major universities secures student housing demand and supports future migration pathways for families.
- **Currency arbitrage:** AUD is historically low vs SGD/HKD/USD/CNY — foreign currency buys more Australian property today than at most points in the last decade.
- **Safe-haven jurisdiction:** AAA-rated sovereign, transparent Torrens title (government-guaranteed), stable legal system, no foreign-exchange restrictions.
- **Deal-specific extras:** some developments offer rental guarantees, land-tax rebates or stamp-duty credits — assess per project.

## 3. Financing & worked example (NSW)

Non-bank specialist lenders typically offer **60–70% LVR** for non-residents. HK/Singapore incomes are commonly accepted by many non-resident lenders; strict capital controls restrict mainland Chinese applicants (a funds-transfer issue, not a lending rule).

**Worked example: $800,000 new apartment, NSW (2026-27)**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| FIRB application fee (< $1M) | $15,600 (indexed 1 July 2026) |
| NSW transfer duty (~$800k) | ~$30,400 |
| Foreign surcharge duty (9%) | $72,000 (verified: Revenue NSW, effective Jan 2025) |
| **Total government upfront costs** | **~$118,000** |
| Typical loan (70% LVR) | $560,000 |
| **Cash/equity at settlement** | **~$358,000** (30% deposit + government costs) |

*Costs vary materially by state — the figures above are NSW-specific; use a state-calculated estimate for other jurisdictions.*

## 4. State strategy (directional labels, not investment advice)

- **WA (7% surcharge):** high-yield play. Up to 100% off-the-plan duty relief (≤ $800k, to 2028) materially reduces upfront capital drag — the best state-level incentive.
- **QLD (8% surcharge):** balanced yield/growth; interstate migration + Olympic infrastructure.
- **NSW (9% surcharge):** highest entry cost, but defensively robust long-term institutional capital growth.
- **ACT / NT (0% surcharge):** lowest tax for foreign investors; smaller markets.

---

## 5. Exit strategy & liquidity

- Property is inherently illiquid vs equities; an indicative **60–90 day exit campaign** (market-dependent), with foreign owners typically selling to local residents.
- **CGT:** foreign residents are ineligible for the 50% CGT discount — budget accordingly.
- **FRCGW:** 15% Foreign Resident Capital Gains Withholding at settlement is a prepayment against final CGT (creditable/refundable on lodgement).
- **Vacancy fee:** dwellings vacant > 183 days incur an annual penalty = **double the FIRB application fee** (e.g. $31,200 on a $15,600-fee property) — keep the property tenanted.

## 6. Global jurisdiction comparison (2025-26)

| Metric | **Australia** | Singapore | Canada | UK | USA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market access | **NEW builds only** | Open | **BANNED (to 2027)** | Open | State bans (e.g. FL) |
| Foreign stamp duty | Standard + 0–9% | Standard + **60%** | Up to 20% | Standard + 2% | State dependent |
| Estate tax | **0%** | 0% | 0% | 40% | 40% (> USD 60k) |
| Withholding on sale | 15% FRCGW (creditable) | N/A | 25–50% (clearance) | N/A | 15% FIRPTA |
| New-dwelling access | **Open + incentivised** | Open + punitive duty | Closed | Open + surcharge | Restricted |

**Read of the table:** Australia is the only jurisdiction where foreign capital is actively *channeled into* new dwellings (ban on established + WA/VIC incentives), while comparator markets either close the door (Canada), tax entry punitively (Singapore), or layer estate/sale taxes on top (US/UK).

## Executive Summary — the five reasons ACT and WA win for Singaporean investors

1. **The 60% arbitrage.** Singapore's Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty of 60% on foreign buyers is the most punitive property tax in the developed world. Against that backdrop, the Australian states with the *lowest* foreign-investor tax burden — the Australian Capital Territory (0% surcharge) and Western Australia (7% surcharge, offset by a 100% off-the-plan duty concession) — offer the steepest reduction in entry cost for Singaporean capital.
2. **Zero-friction entry in the ACT.** The ACT is one of only two jurisdictions in Australia (with the Northern Territory) that charges **no foreign buyer surcharge at all** — neither surcharge purchaser duty nor surcharge land tax. For a Singaporean investor, that is a direct saving of up to 9% of the purchase price compared with NSW.
3. **The cheapest new-property entry in WA.** WA's foreign surcharge is 7% — the lowest of the mainland states — and its **off-the-plan duty concession removes 100% of transfer duty on new dwellings up to $800,000** (capped at $50,000), extended to 30 June 2028. No other state offers a comparable new-build incentive.
4. **Demand fundamentals are the strongest in the country.** WA recorded the fastest population growth of any Australian state (2.2% in the year to December 2025, ABS) with Perth's rental vacancy among the tightest in the nation. The ACT's government-anchored economy delivers exceptional rental demand stability.
5. **New builds are the only lawful door.** The federal ban on foreign purchases of established dwellings (1 April 2025 – 30 June 2029) channels all foreign investment into new property — precisely the segment where ACT and WA offer the strongest incentives. Singaporean investors are legally directed to the product class these two states reward most.

## 1. The arbitrage: Singapore's 60% ABSD versus Australia's 0–9%

Singapore imposes a 60% Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty on foreign individuals purchasing residential property — on top of the standard buyer's stamp duty. A $1 million Singapore purchase attracts approximately $600,000 in ABSD alone.

Australia's state-level foreign surcharges range from **0% (ACT, NT) to 9% (NSW)**. Even at the most expensive Australian jurisdiction, a Singaporean investor pays a fraction of the entry tax they face at home. At the best jurisdictions — ACT and WA — the differential is stark:

| Jurisdiction | Foreign surcharge duty | Foreign surcharge land tax | Off-the-plan concession |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACT** | **0%** | **0%** | — |
| **WA** | **7%** | **0%** | **100% duty relief ≤ $800k (to 2028)** |
| NT | 0% | 0% | — |
| SA | 8% | 4% (from 2025-26) | — |
| TAS | 8% | 2% | — |
| QLD | 8% | 3% | — |
| VIC | 8% | 4% (absentee) | Off-the-plan construction deduction (to Oct 2026) |
| NSW | 9% | 5% | — |

*Sources: state revenue offices, verified 6 August 2026.*

The AUD's historically low value against the SGD compounds the advantage: a Singaporean investor's currency buys more Australian property today than at most points in the past decade, and Australian property is priced in a free-float currency with no capital controls on repatriation of sale proceeds.

## 2. The only lawful door: the new-dwelling pathway

Under the *Foreign Acquisitions and Takeovers Act*, foreign persons are **banned from purchasing established dwellings from 1 April 2025 to 30 June 2029** (extended from an earlier 31 March 2027 expiry; ATO). The only lawful pathway for most foreign non-residents is:

- **New dwellings** — never occupied, never previously sold (off-the-plan and near-new)
- **Vacant land with an approved development plan** — construction must commence within 24 months
- Purchases within developments holding **New/Near-New Dwelling Exemption Certificates** (streamlined approval)

FIRB approval is a one-off application: **$15,600** for a dwelling up to $1 million (2026-27, indexed from $15,100 in 2025-26), $31,300 up to $2 million. 50% is refundable if the purchase falls through.

**The strategic implication:** the ban makes *new property the entire addressable market* for foreign investors. It is government policy — not marketing — that directs foreign capital into new developments. The states that actively incentivise new builds (WA above all) convert this regulatory reality into a financial advantage.

## 3. The ranking framework

Each jurisdiction is scored across six dimensions for the *Singaporean* investor:

1. **Foreign-entry cost** — surcharge duty + surcharge land tax burden
2. **New-dwelling incentives** — off-the-plan concessions, rebates, deferrals
3. **Rental yield** — gross yield and vacancy tightness
4. **Capital-growth outlook** — population growth, undersupply, pipeline
5. **Liquidity and exit depth** — market size, resale depth, investor demand
6. **Tax-regime simplicity** — land tax, absentee provisions, compliance burden

## 4. Ranked #1 — Western Australia: the performance leader

WA ranks first on the composite score, driven by the strongest demand fundamentals in Australia combined with the single most valuable state incentive for new property.

**The numbers that matter:**

- **Population growth 2.2% in the year to December 2025** — the fastest of any Australian state (ABS, National State and Territory Population, December 2025). WA added 65,500 people; net overseas migration of 40,422 was the largest contributor.
- **Rental vacancy ~2.0%** (REIWA measure, mid-2026; SQM Research reports even tighter at 0.6–0.8%) — a landlord's market with rents at record highs. Every capital city vacancy rate sits below 2.0% nationally, but Perth remains among the tightest.
- **Undersupply:** six of Australia's ten fastest-growing LGAs are in WA (ABS 2024-25 regional population data) — Kwinana (+4.6%), Serpentine-Jarrahdale (+4.5%) and Swan (+4.0%) leading.

**The entry-cost story — the decisive factor for Singaporeans:**

| $700,000 new apartment, off-the-plan | WA | ACT | QLD (comparison) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transfer duty | **$0** (100% OTP concession) | ~$19,300 | ~$24,500 |
| Foreign surcharge duty | $49,000 (7%) | **$0** (0%) | $56,000 (8%) |
| **Total state entry tax** | **$49,000** | **$19,300** | $80,500 |
| Foreign land tax (annual) | 0% | 0% | 3% |

*WA's $0 duty outcome relies on the Off-The-Plan concession: 100% of transfer duty for new dwellings ≤ $800,000, capped at $50,000, valid 12 March 2026 – 30 June 2028, sliding to 50% between $800k–$900k. Concession rules are conditional (pre-construction contracts) and subject to legislative change — obtain independent legal and tax validation before exchange.*

**Honest caveats:** WA's economy carries commodity-cycle exposure; capital growth is more volatile than the eastern states' long-run averages; and the distance from Sydney/Melbourne markets means weaker east-coast buyer depth. The resource-driven demand base, however, has translated into sustained migration and one of the tightest rental markets in the country.

## 5. Ranked #2 — ACT: the tax-efficiency leader

The ACT ranks second — and first on pure tax efficiency — because it is one of only two Australian jurisdictions with **zero foreign surcharges** (0% surcharge purchaser duty, 0% surcharge land tax).

**The numbers that matter:**

- **Rental demand stability:** Canberra's employment base is anchored by the Commonwealth public sector — the most counter-cyclical employer in Australia. Vacancy has historically run among the tightest of capital cities (SQM Research, June 2026: Canberra 1.7%).
- **Gross yields:** Canberra new-build apartment yields (~5–6%) are among the highest of the capital cities, supported by the rental-guarantee structure common in off-the-plan developments.
- **0% surcharge = maximum upfront capital preservation.** On a $700,000 purchase, the ACT saves the Singaporean investor $49,000–63,000 in surcharge duty compared with WA (7%) or NSW (9%).

**Honest caveats — the supply pipeline:** the ACT's future apartment pipeline is substantial. The HIA reports ACT dwelling commencements surged **71.8% in the March 2026 quarter, driven almost entirely by apartments**, and multi-unit dwellings dominate scheduled land releases under the territory's new planning framework ("missing middle" reforms took effect 1 July 2026). A heavily supplied apartment pipeline can suppress short-term capital growth and rental yield growth — the investor should target sites with genuine scarcity (location, view, quality) rather than volume product.

The ACT is the superior choice for a conservative allocation seeking maximum capital preservation and predictable rental income.

## 8. Risks and compliance obligations

- **FIRB application fee:** $15,600 (≤ $1M, 2026-27) — one-off, 50% refundable if the purchase falls through.
- **Vacancy fee:** if a foreign-owned dwelling is vacant (or not genuinely available for rent) for more than 183 days a year, an annual fee of **double the FIRB application fee** applies.
- **Capital gains tax:** foreign residents are **ineligible for the 50% CGT discount**. At settlement, a **15% Foreign Resident Capital Gains Withholding** applies — a creditable prepayment against the final liability, not an additional tax.
- **Income tax:** rental income is taxed at Australian non-resident marginal rates (~30–45% depending on income level); negative gearing is permitted, with losses offsetting Australian-sourced income. New builds maximise Division 43 capital-works deductions (2.5% per year) and plant-and-equipment depreciation.
- **No estate tax:** Australia imposes no estate or inheritance tax — an unrestricted generational wealth transfer, unlike the US (40% above USD 60k) or the UK (40%).
- **Lending:** non-resident borrowers typically access 60–70% LVR through specialist non-bank lenders. Singaporean income documentation is commonly accepted by many non-resident lenders.
- **Double-tax treaty:** the Australia–Singapore DTA allocates primary property-taxing rights to Australia, with home-country foreign tax credits preventing double taxation.

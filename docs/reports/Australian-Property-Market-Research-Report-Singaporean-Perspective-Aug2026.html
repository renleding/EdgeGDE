Introduction

Australia's property market — the context for Singaporean buyers

For a Singaporean purchaser, the starting comparison is stark. Singapore's Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty charges foreign buyers 60% of a property's value. In Australia's Australian Capital Territory the equivalent foreign surcharge is 0%, and in Western Australia a 100% off-the-plan duty concession removes the standard transfer duty on new dwellings up to $800,000.

The federal ban on foreign purchases of established dwellings (1 April 2025 – 30 June 2029) means that, for most foreign non-residents, new dwellings represent the primary pathway available under current FIRB rules (limited exceptions apply). This report examines the state-level environment — surcharges, incentives, yields, population growth and market depth — for purchasers of new property, and applies a transparent scoring framework to compare the eight Australian jurisdictions.

Key Observations

What the data shows

Applying the weighted scoring framework in Section 3, Western Australia recorded the highest composite score among the eight jurisdictions, and the Australian Capital Territory the second-highest. The observations below summarise the data behind those scores.

1 — The ABSD differential

Singapore's ABSD of 60% on foreign buyers is among the highest property-transfer taxes in the developed world. Australia's state-level foreign surcharges range from 0% (ACT, NT) to 9% (NSW). The ACT (0%) and WA (7%, offset by the 100% duty concession) exhibit the lowest combined foreign-investor entry costs.

2 — ACT: the zero-surcharge jurisdiction

The ACT is one of two jurisdictions (with the Northern Territory) with no foreign surcharge — neither surcharge purchaser duty nor surcharge land tax. On a $700,000 purchase, the ACT's zero surcharge compares with $49,000 in WA and $63,000 in NSW.

3 — WA: the off-the-plan duty concession

WA's 7% surcharge is the lowest of the mainland states, and its off-the-plan duty concession removes 100% of transfer duty on new dwellings up to $800,000 (capped $50,000, extended to 30 June 2028) — the only concession of its kind among Australian states.

4 — Demand fundamentals

WA recorded the fastest population growth of any state (2.2% in the year to December 2025, ABS), with Perth's rental vacancy among the tightest in the nation (REIWA ~2.0%; SQM Research 0.6%). The ACT's government-anchored economy provides a stable employment and rental base (SQM Research, June 2026: Canberra 1.7%).

5 — The new-dwelling pathway

For most foreign non-residents, the 2025–29 established-dwelling ban directs investment toward new property — the segment in which ACT and WA exhibit the strongest state-level incentives. FIRB approval is a one-off application ($15,600 for dwellings up to $1M, 2026-27).

Section 2

The purchase pathway for foreign non-residents

Under the Foreign Acquisitions and Takeovers Act, foreign persons are generally prohibited from purchasing established dwellings from 1 April 2025 to 30 June 2029 (extended from an earlier 31 March 2027 expiry; ATO). For most foreign non-resident investors, new dwellings represent the primary pathway available under current FIRB rules; limited exceptions and special structures exist.

The principal pathways are:

  • New dwellings — never occupied, never previously sold (off-the-plan and near-new);
  • Vacant land with an approved development plan — construction must commence within 24 months;
  • Purchases within developments holding New/Near-New Dwelling Exemption Certificates (streamlined approval).

FIRB approval is a one-off application: $15,600 for a dwelling up to $1 million (2026-27, indexed from $15,100 in 2025-26), $31,300 up to $2 million, with 50% refundable if the purchase does not proceed. Eligibility under FIRB rules is case-specific — readers should confirm their position with an adviser.

Western Australia

WA — highest composite score under the framework

Western Australia recorded the highest composite score: the strongest population-growth data in Australia combined with the most valuable state incentive for new property.

Entry cost

WA's $0 duty outcome relies on the Off-The-Plan concession: 100% of transfer duty for new dwellings ≤ $800,000, capped at $50,000, valid 12 March 2026 – 30 June 2028, sliding to 50% between $800k–$900k. Concession rules are conditional (pre-construction contracts) and subject to legislative change — obtain independent legal and tax validation before exchange.

Considerations: WA's economy carries commodity-cycle exposure; capital growth has historically been more volatile than the eastern states' long-run averages; and distance from the Sydney–Melbourne corridor means a different buyer profile. The resource-driven demand base has translated into sustained migration and one of the tightest rental markets in the country (Perth vacancy ~0.6–2.0%).

Australian Capital Territory

ACT — lowest foreign-investor entry costs

The ACT recorded the second-highest composite score and the lowest foreign-investor entry costs: it is one of only two Australian jurisdictions with zero foreign surcharges (0% surcharge purchaser duty, 0% surcharge land tax).

Demand stability

A government-anchored economy

Canberra's employment base is anchored by the Commonwealth public sector — a stable, counter-cyclical employer. Vacancy ~1.7% (SQM, June 2026).

Entry cost

Zero-surcharge jurisdiction

0% surcharge duty and 0% surcharge land tax. On a $700,000 purchase, the ACT's entry cost is lower than WA (7%) and materially lower than NSW (9%).

Supply considerations: the ACT's future apartment pipeline is substantial. HIA reports ACT dwelling commencements surged 71.8% in the March 2026 quarter, driven almost entirely by apartments, and multi-unit dwellings dominate scheduled land releases under the territory's planning framework. A heavily supplied pipeline may affect short-term rental and price dynamics — site selection (location, view, quality) is a material factor.

Vacancy feeIf a foreign-owned dwelling is vacant (or not genuinely available for rent) more than 183 days a year, an annual fee of double the FIRB application fee applies.
Capital gains taxForeign residents are ineligible for the 50% CGT discount. A 15% Foreign Resident Capital Gains Withholding applies at settlement — a creditable prepayment, not an additional tax.
